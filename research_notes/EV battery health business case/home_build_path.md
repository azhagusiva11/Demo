## 1. How did existing SoH startups get their first customers and data, and how long did it take to reach first revenue?

### Takeaway
Every comparable company got going through one distribution partner that already had trusted traffic: an auto club (Aviloo with ADAC and ACS, and already with ACL in Luxembourg), a dealer group or auction (Generational with Carbase and SVA; Moba with Arnold Clark, Aramisauto and Emil Frey), or a small set of paying specialist dealers (Recurrent). Most took about 1 to 2+ years from founding to a product on the market. Venture-studio-backed Recurrent was the fast exception: it had paying dealers the year it was founded.

### Inferences
- **Repeatable playbook:** (1) build a test that works on the most common local models; (2) sign one channel partner that already has trust and traffic (auto club, testing body, dealer group, auction or leasing company); (3) use that partner's volume to collect validation data; (4) get a recognised approval (TÜV, CARA, a university) before approaching large dealer groups, which run competitive reviews (Arnold Clark did).
- **Time to first revenue:** about 12–18 months for bootstrapped or small-seed teams (Generational: late 2023 founding to Feb 2025 launch; Aviloo: about 2 years of R&D). Recurrent was faster because a studio funded it and it relied on existing car APIs rather than its own hardware.
- **The ACL route is taken in Luxembourg.** ACL already resells Aviloo, so a newcomer's likely first channels are:
  - a dealer group
  - a leasing company's remarketing desk (Moba's win at Arval is a precedent)
  - used-car platforms
  - insurers or banks
- The Generational founders (F1 vehicle-data background, two people) are the closest skill match to this founder's CAN/VBOX profile. They still took about 15 months to launch. As a solo founder, plan for at least that long.

## 2. Business models and unit economics

### Takeaway
There are four models in use: consumer tests (about €79–€99), B2B per-test fees with a hardware licence (about €30–€35 per test plus about €100–€480 per year or one-off for kit), dealer software subscriptions ($50–250/month), and API/telematics analytics for fleets. B2B per-test prices are now in the "low tens of pounds/euros", and a basic SoH reading is being commoditised by $30–$140 dongles.

### Cited Findings
- **Aviloo B2B:** "an annual license fee for the AVILOO box (€480 / $550) and then it's €35 ($40) per test" (from a search snippet, probably quoting the Forbes piece; not fetched) — [Forbes, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jamesmorris/2026/07/29/how-aviloo-is-solving-the-used-ev-market-battery-health-trust-problem/)
- **Aviloo consumer:** Premium test €99 through ADAC — [ADAC Württemberg](https://www.adac.de/der-adac/regionalclubs/wuerttemberg/elektromobilitaet/aviloo/). A reseller in Australia/New Zealand sells the Flash test from AUD 200 — [TestEV](https://testev.co/)
- **Generational:**
  - Kits cost under £100, and tests are "broadly mid/low tens of pounds per test as an entry point, and that number can come down with volume" — [Business Car](https://www.businesscar.co.uk/analysis/remarketing/remarketing-battery-health-in-focus/)
  - Tests start at £28.50 and fall with volume, with no monthly commitment. The kit is £95 plus a £100 setup fee (attribution uncertain between Generational's own site and the Business Car article) — [Generational](https://generational.ac/)
  - The test runs "in seconds" once the kit is connected — same sources
- **Resale margin:** an independent mobile tester in West Yorkshire charges £90 per test for battery health checks. Its supplier is not confirmed. Against a £28.50 wholesale per-test fee, that implies roughly 3x retail markup — [EV Battery Health Checker](https://evbatteryhealthchecker.co.uk/)
- **Moba:** diagnostic device €59 incl. VAT; certification kit with digital certificate €79 (excl. shipping) — [Moba kit](https://get-moba.com/produit/kit-de-certification/)
- **Recurrent:** dealer subscription of $50–250/month by inventory size at launch — [GeekWire 2020](https://www.geekwire.com/2020/new-startup-recurrent-brings-transparency-ev-batteries-reducing-risk-used-car-shopping/)
- **DEKRA:** a 15-minute test. An OBD dongle reads static data, then the car accelerates from standstill to about 40 km/h with measuring equipment fitted. The algorithm is patented and validated by RWTH Aachen — [DEKRA](https://www.dekra.com/en/battery-test-for-electric-cars/)
- **TÜV Rheinland and TWAICE** set up a joint venture, "Battery Quick Check GmbH", for certified battery reports — [TÜV Rheinland](https://www.tuv.com/press/en/press-releases/newsroom-detail-page_373445.html)
- **Commoditisation pressure:**
  - "A $30 OBD-II dongle and the right app give most EV owners the same number a workshop charges $99 to read." But a generic $20 reader "will return almost nothing on an EV" without manufacturer-specific protocols — [iEVChina guide](https://ievchina.com/insights/ev-battery-health-check-soh-guide/)
  - Free SoH apps exist, for example VoltChek — [VoltChek](https://voltchek.app/hardware)

### Inferences
- **Indicative unit economics for a Luxembourg solo operator**, using the cited price points:
  - Dealer or leasing work would likely clear about €25–€40 per test, because Aviloo (€35) and Generational (~£28.50) set the reference.
  - Consumers would pay about €79–€99, because Moba (€79) and Aviloo via ADAC/ACL (€99) set the reference.
  - The marginal cost of an OBD-read test is near zero (cloud compute plus the tester's time). Any drive-cycle or technician-visit test mainly costs labour and travel.
- **Volume needed to live on it:** €60k/year of gross margin at about €30 net per B2B test means about 2,000 tests a year. At €80 per consumer test it means about 750. In a country of Luxembourg's size (see §3), those volumes are a significant share of local used-EV transactions. That points either to cross-border sales in the Greater Region or to a software/licensing model rather than technician visits.
- **Hybrid option:** start as a paid local operator or reseller of an established test (Generational offers no-commitment kits; Moba sells kits). That brings in revenue and contacts while the founder builds and validates their own method. The cost is dependence on the supplier and thin margins.

### Gaps
- Current Aviloo, DEKRA and TÜV Rheinland/TWAICE list prices in Luxembourg, Belgium or France were not retrieved. ACL's price for the Aviloo test in Luxembourg was not in the snippets.
- No sourced gross-margin figures from any SoH company.
- Recurrent's current (2026) dealer or API pricing was not retrieved.

## 3. Luxembourg: potential customers and market size

### Takeaway
Luxembourg adds roughly 45–50k new cars a year. Battery-electric cars were about 29.5% of Q1 2025 registrations, and company cars are 54% of new registrations, with about 47% of EVs leased. Leasing companies and dealer groups therefore control most of the future used-EV supply. ACL already offers the Aviloo test, so the open channels are leasing remarketing, dealer groups, used-car platforms and insurers.

### Cited Findings
**Registrations and fleet**
- 12,671 cars were registered in Luxembourg in Q1 2025. Battery-electric cars had a 29.5% share (vs 23% a year earlier). Electrified cars overall (BEV, hybrid and plug-in hybrid) were 64.7% — [Paperjam](https://en.paperjam.lu/article/12-671-cars-registered-in-luxembourg-in-q1-2025)
- 2024 year to date: BEVs had a 27% market share, and 10.6% of the passenger-car (M1) fleet was rechargeable — [Chambre de Commerce, "Voitures électriques au Luxembourg" (PDF)](https://www.cc.lu/fileadmin/user_upload/tx_ccnews/AT_28_electromobilite.pdf)
- EV registrations in H1 2026 rose 21% vs 2025, against 3.5% for the whole fleet, per the Ministry of Mobility (attribution uncertain between Infogreen and Paperjam) — [Infogreen](https://www.infogreen.lu/les-voitures-100-electriques-en-forte-hausse-au-luxembourg.html)
- Also reported, with uncertain attribution: EVs were 10.79% of the registered fleet as of Jan 2025, and 33.7% of new registrations in Jan–Sep 2025 were electric — [Plug-in EVs in Luxembourg (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_electric_vehicles_in_Luxembourg); [EV TCP Luxembourg](https://evtcp.org/country/luxembourg/)
- **Conflict:** one snippet said Luxembourg's EV market "fell 17.5% in 2025 to a 10% share" — [Focus2move](https://www.focus2move.com/luxembourg-car-market/). This contradicts the Paperjam Q1 2025 figure (29.5% BEV) and the Jan–Sep 2025 figure (33.7%). It is probably a misreading; treat it as unreliable.

**Company cars and leasing**
- Company cars are 54% of new car registrations, and 47.03% of electric cars in Luxembourg are leased. Until the end of 2026, electric company cars keep a benefit-in-kind rate of 0.5–0.6%, against 2% for combustion and hybrid cars — [Leasys Luxembourg](https://www.leasys.com/lu/english/blog/leasing_company_cars_in_luxembourg:_a_turning_point_in_2025)
- Leasing players:
  - Ayvens Luxembourg (formerly ALD/LeasePlan) — [Ayvens LU](https://www.ayvens.com/en-lu/)
  - Arval Luxembourg (in the market since 1997) — [Global Fleet marketplace](https://marketplace.globalfleet.com/marketplace/company/arval-luxembourg)
  - Arval acquired Athlon (about 400,000 vehicles across Europe), which strengthens Arval in Luxembourg among other markets — [Fleet Europe](https://www.fleeteurope.com/en/financial-models/europe/news/arval-acquires-athlon-creating-new-european-leasing-giant)
  - Leasys also operates in Luxembourg — [Leasys LU](https://www.leasys.com/lu/english/blog/leasing_company_cars_in_luxembourg:_a_turning_point_in_2025)
- Arval is already a Moba customer elsewhere in Europe — [AM Online](https://www.am-online.com/news/arnold-clark-adds-ev-battery-health-checks-with-moba-partnership)

- ACL runs a Diagnostic Center and offers the Aviloo battery test (contact +352 450045, dc1@acl.lu) — [ACL](https://www.acl.lu/en/mobility/vehicle-diagnostics/)
- Used-car channels in Luxembourg:
  - Autopolis used-vehicle centre — [Autopolis](https://www.autopolis.lu/centre-de-vehicules-d-occasion)
  - Luxauto.lu — [Luxauto](https://www.luxauto.lu/fr)
  - AutoScout24.lu — [AutoScout24](https://www.autoscout24.lu/)
  - Renew (lu.renew.auto) — [Renew](https://lu.renew.auto/)
  - DriveMarket — [DriveMarket](https://www.drive-market.lu/en/)
  - Automarket.lu — [Automarket](https://www.automarket.lu/en/)

### Inferences
- **Addressable volume (rough):**
  - About 12.7k registrations per quarter implies roughly 45–50k new cars a year, of which about 13–15k are battery-electric at a ~30% share.
  - With 54% company cars and typical 3–4 year leases (the lease length is an assumption, not sourced here), off-lease BEVs from the 2022–2025 registration peak return to the used market from about 2026–2029. That wave is the timing window for a SoH service aimed at leasing remarketing.
- **Target list by likelihood of a first "yes":**
  1. A mid-size dealer group's used-car centre (e.g., Autopolis), sold as SoH certificates to support used-EV sales
  2. A leasing company's end-of-lease and remarketing process (Ayvens, Arval, Leasys). These are pan-European buyers, so they may already have group-level contracts (e.g., Arval–Moba).
  3. Used-car platforms (Luxauto, AutoScout24.lu, Renew), sold as a "battery certified" listing badge or white-label report
  4. Insurers (Foyer, Lalux) and banks, for EV loan residual-value risk
- **ACL is a competitor-partner.** It already resells Aviloo, so pitching ACL makes sense only for a clearly different product, such as fleet or telematics analytics.
- Luxembourg is small, so the realistic market is the Greater Region (Lorraine, Saarland, Rhineland-Palatinate/Trier, Wallonia). Its cross-border dealer and leasing groups could spread a Luxembourg reference account.

## 4. Luxembourg business setup and support programs

### Takeaway
A solo founder can incorporate a SARL-S (€1 capital, no notary) for roughly €430–€1,500. Add a business permit (€50 fee, processed in up to 3 months, needing good repute, qualification where required, and a real establishment in Luxembourg) and about 25–28% self-employed social contributions (minimum around €225–240/month). **Fit 4 Start (up to €150k, equity-free) is not currently a clean fit:** edition #17 (closed 10 July 2026) covered only Digital, Health tech, Defence and Space, so an SoH startup would have to apply as "Digital". The Automobility Incubator in Bissen, run by Technoport, is the natural base for a sector-specific incubator.

### Cited Findings
**Legal form**
- A SARL-S has share capital between €1 and €11,999 (introduced 2016). It can be formed by private deed using the business register's (RCS) model articles, without a notary. Registration with the RCS and publication in RESA (the official gazette) are still mandatory — [Fiduciaire-Expert](https://fiduciaire-expert.lu/en/articles/sarl-s-luxembourg-simplified-limited-liability-company/)
- A standard SARL needs a notary, typically €1,000–€2,500 — same source
- **Formation cost of a SARL-S:**
  - About €430: €1 capital + ~€50 RCS + ~€50 business permit + ~€200 non-bankruptcy declaration + €0 VAT registration + ~€130 LuxTrust Pro (attribution uncertain) — [Odysya](https://odysya.io/guides/sarl-s-cost-luxembourg); [Fiduciaire-Expert](https://fiduciaire-expert.lu/en/articles/sarl-s-luxembourg-simplified-limited-liability-company/)
  - Another guide puts it at about €1,500 without a notary, against €5,000–€10,000 for a standard SARL or SA — [Bookkeeper.lu](https://bookkeeper.lu/en/blog/company-formation-cost-luxembourg)

**Business permit (autorisation d'établissement)**
- Three cumulative conditions: professional qualification, the manager's good repute, and a real establishment in Luxembourg — [Guichet.lu – commercial activities](http://www.guichet.public.lu/entreprises/en/creation-developpement/autorisation-etablissement/commerce/commerce/index.html)
- Good repute means a clean criminal record extract under 3 months old. Qualification, depending on the activity, means a diploma, at least 3 years' sector experience, or a certificate. Establishment means commercial premises or a domiciliation contract — [Advena](https://advena.lu/en/journal/autorisation-detablissement-au-luxembourg-conditions-demarches-et-delais); [Easybiz](https://easybiz.lu/en/blog/how-to-obtain-a-business-permit-in-luxembourg)
- €50 chancellery fee. Online through MyGuichet.lu (with LuxTrust or eIDAS login) since May 2024. Processed in principle within 3 months, and silence on a complete file counts as approval — [Fiduciaire-Expert](https://fiduciaire-expert.lu/fr/articles/autorisation-etablissement/)

**Social security for the self-employed (2026)**
- Total contribution rate of about 25–28% of professional income, paying both employer and employee shares. The pension rate rose from 16% to 17% on 1 Jan 2026 — [Bookkeeper.lu](https://bookkeeper.lu/en/blog/cotisations-sociales-independant-luxembourg); [TaxRavens](https://taxravens.com/en/luxembourg/social-contributions)
- Minimum contribution base: 1/3 of the unskilled minimum wage (€901.25/month in 2026), giving roughly €225–€240/month in contributions. Sources describe it inconsistently for main vs secondary activity. The official page is [CCSS contribution base](https://ccss.public.lu/en/independants/cotisations-sociales/assiette-cotisation-adaptation.html) (not fetched) — [BravoLisa](https://wiki.bravolisa.lu/en/article/ccss-for-self-employed-in-luxembourg-how-it-works-10cu6wg/); [Bookkeeper.lu](https://bookkeeper.lu/en/blog/devenir-independant-luxembourg-guide)

## 5. Home-build costs and time

### Takeaway
The hardware to prototype is cheap: an OBDLink MX+ at about $140 and a CSS CANedge logger at about €580 cover OBD/UDS reads and CAN logging. Company setup is about €430–€1,500. So cash to a minimum viable product is probably in the low thousands of euros. The real constraints are access to many different EV models for validation, and founder time (about 12–18 months to launch, based on peers).

### Cited Findings
- **OBDLink MX+:** $139.95 — [OBDLink](https://www.obdlink.com/products/obdlink-mxp/). The OBDLink MX+ with a Tesla CAN cable is described as the de facto standard for Tesla — [iEVChina](https://ievchina.com/insights/ev-battery-health-check-soh-guide/) (attribution uncertain)
- **CSS Electronics CANedge3** (2x CAN/LIN, 4G, GPS/IMU): €580. It can log raw CAN, OBD2 and UDS data. The CANedge1/2 are the same without the cellular link — [CSS Electronics CANedge3](https://www.csselectronics.com/products/can-bus-data-logger-4g-lte-canedge3-gnss); [CANedge2](https://www.csselectronics.com/products/can-bus-data-logger-wifi-canedge2)
- CSS Electronics sells an EV/battery telemetry setup — [CSS EV data logger](https://www.csselectronics.com/pages/electric-vehicle-data-logger-cloud-battery-telematics)
- Published framework for low-cost OBD-II data logging on battery-electric vehicles — [MDPI Vehicles 4(4):64](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-8921/4/4/64/xml)
- Generic $20 readers return almost nothing on an EV. Manufacturer-specific protocols are needed — [iEVChina](https://ievchina.com/insights/ev-battery-health-check-soh-guide/)
- Aviloo's consumer test needs a drive from 100% to 10% charge with the box plugged in. The B2B flash test takes about 3 minutes. DEKRA's test takes 15 minutes including an acceleration to 40 km/h. This shows the design trade-off between accuracy and throughput — [ACL](https://www.acl.lu/en/mobility/vehicle-diagnostics/); [GEWINN](https://www.gewinn.com/artikel/vom-start-up-zum-standard); [DEKRA](https://www.dekra.com/en/battery-test-for-electric-cars/)

## 6. Credibility levers

### Takeaway
Credibility in this market comes from third-party validation. The pattern is: testing and certification bodies (TÜV SÜD–Aviloo, TÜV Nord–volytica, TÜV Rheinland–TWAICE, and DEKRA's own test), university validation (DEKRA with RWTH Aachen), accelerator or industry approvals (Moba via Moove Lab; CARA approval for Moba and Generational in the UK), and matching the manufacturer's warranty SoH. No evidence was found that any SoH startup relies on ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation as its main credential.

### Cited Findings
- DEKRA's patented algorithm is "validated by RWTH Aachen University" — [DEKRA](https://www.dekra.com/en/battery-test-for-electric-cars/)
- TÜV Rheinland and TWAICE formed a joint venture (Battery Quick Check GmbH) for TÜV-certified battery reports — [TÜV Rheinland](https://www.tuv.com/press/en/press-releases/newsroom-detail-page_373445.html); [TWAICE](https://www.twaice.com/newsroom/tuv-rheinland-certified-battery-test-for-battery-electric-vehicles-creates-trust-on-the-used-car-market)
- TÜV SÜD featured the Aviloo professional test — [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-us/e-ssentials-newsletter/automotive-essentials/e-ssentials-03-2022/aviloo-battery-test-for-professionals)
- TÜV Nord worked with volytica on the e-bus assessment (attribution uncertain) — [volytica](https://www.volytica.com/solutions). Volytica holds ISO 27001 (information security) — [volytica](https://www.volytica.com/about/)
- Moba Certify Pro was the first "CARA Approved" solution, confirming that its SoH "matches the manufacturer's SOH, which the battery warranty depends on" — [Moba Certify Pro](https://get-moba.com/en/moba-certify-pro-en/). Generational is also CARA Approved — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/generational-ac_generational-is-now-battery-health-check-activity-7489961079222022144-3iQW)
- Moba had its analysis system certified through the Moove Lab accelerator — [Moove Lab](https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/moove-lab_moba-fait-certifier-son-syst%C3%A8me-danalyse-activity-7074777509526937602-Ip4x)
- Luxembourg research partner: LIST coordinates the Horizon Europe project OPINCHARGE (€5M from the EU plus €0.7M from Switzerland), which develops tools to study interfaces in lithium-ion batteries. It is materials-level research, not vehicle SoH analytics — [LIST](https://www.list.lu/en/news/towards-safer-and-more-sustainable-lithium-ion-batteries/?no_cache=1&cHash=864ca294e9ede349653316f96dd6c92f)

### Inferences
- **Most credible low-cost path for a solo founder:**
  1. Publish an open validation study comparing the method's SoH with the car's own BMS SoH and with measured capacity from full charge/discharge, on at least 30 vehicles.
  2. Run it with a Luxembourg research partner: LIST or the University of Luxembourg, possibly through Luxinnovation's R&D and innovation schemes.
  3. Only then approach a testing body (TÜV, DEKRA, SGS or the national body) about co-branding.
- The UK's CARA approval shows that dealer and remarketing buyers value alignment with manufacturer warranty SoH. The report should state the warranty threshold (often 70%) for each car. That threshold is a general industry convention, not verified here.
- ISO/IEC 17025 is probably overkill at launch: slow and costly for a solo operator. Consider it only if SoH certificates become legally required or leasing contracts demand accredited reports.

### Gaps
- No sourced cost or timeline for ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation through Luxembourg's OLAS/ILNAS.
- No University of Luxembourg battery or electromobility group was identified in the search results.
- No sourced evidence of LIST work on vehicle SoH analytics specifically.
- The standard behind "CARA Approved" and what the acronym means were not confirmed.

## 7. Key risks and failure modes for small players

### Takeaway
The biggest risks for a Luxembourg solo entrant are:
- **an established incumbent already in the local auto club** (Aviloo via ACL)
- **big testing bodies** (DEKRA, TÜV Rheinland–TWAICE) with their own tests
- **price compression** to about €30/test B2B and €59–€99 B2C
- **regulation**: EU Battery Regulation Article 14 makes BMS SoH data readable, which commoditises simple reads
- **the maintenance burden of vehicle coverage** (manufacturer-specific protocols)
- **a very small domestic market**

### Cited Findings
- The incumbent is present: ACL offers the Aviloo test in Luxembourg — [ACL](https://www.acl.lu/en/mobility/vehicle-diagnostics/)
- Large testing bodies have their own products: DEKRA's 15-minute test validated by RWTH Aachen — [DEKRA](https://www.dekra.com/en/battery-test-for-electric-cars/); the TÜV Rheinland–TWAICE joint venture — [TÜV Rheinland](https://www.tuv.com/press/en/press-releases/newsroom-detail-page_373445.html)
- **Price compression:**
  - Moba kit €79 — [Moba](https://get-moba.com/produit/kit-de-certification/)
  - Generational from £28.50/test — [Generational](https://generational.ac/)
  - Aviloo €35/test B2B — [Forbes via search snippet](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jamesmorris/2026/07/29/how-aviloo-is-solving-the-used-ev-market-battery-health-trust-problem/)
  - A $30 dongle plus an app gives owners "the same number a workshop charges $99 to read" — [iEVChina](https://ievchina.com/insights/ev-battery-health-check-soh-guide/)
- **Competitive selection:** Arnold Clark picked Moba after "a review of several battery diagnostic solutions", deciding on ease of use, speed, vehicle coverage and value — [AM Online](https://www.am-online.com/news/arnold-clark-adds-ev-battery-health-checks-with-moba-partnership)
- **Regulation:** from 18 Aug 2024, EV battery management systems must hold up-to-date SoH and expected-lifetime data (Annex VII), with read-only access for the lawful purchaser — [UL Solutions](https://www.ul.com/insights/industry-insights-eu-battery-regulation-20231542); [batteryregulation.eu](https://www.batteryregulation.eu/post/eu-battery-regulation-2023-1542-2024-requirements)
  - The counterpoint (an opportunity): the carmakers' association ACEA argued that "accessing raw BMS data is not a reliable way of determining the state of health... algorithms used differ", so direct comparison across brands is impossible — [ACEA position paper, Oct 2021](https://www.acea.auto/files/ACEA_position_paper-EU-Batteries-Regulation.pdf)
  - A battery passport will carry SoH data — [Battery passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_passport)
- **Coverage and integration effort:** generic readers fail on EVs, so manufacturer-specific protocols are needed — [iEVChina](https://ievchina.com/insights/ev-battery-health-check-soh-guide/). Recurrent avoided building per-carmaker integrations by using Smartcar ("6 to 12 months and millions of dollars") — [Smartcar](https://smartcar.com/blog/case-study-recurrent)
- **Capital intensity of the leaders:**
  - Recurrent $3.5M seed + $16M Series A — [Recurrent](https://www.recurrentauto.com/news/recurrent-raises-3-5m-seed-round-to-provide-electric-vehicle-battery-reports); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/seattle-startup-recurrent-raises-16m-aims-to-be-standard-for-scoring-used-ev-batteries/)
  - Volytica €5.5M — [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2023/09/dresden-based-volytica-closes-e5-5-million-to-refine-battery-analytics-ahead-of-new-era-of-electrification/)
  - Aviloo €1.25M crowdfunding — [brutkasten](https://brutkasten.com/aviloo-green-rocket/)
  - Moba targeting €1.8M with Engie as shareholder — [Journal Auto](https://journalauto.com/services/batteries-for-people-devient-moba/)
- **Consolidation:** Elysia (Fortescue) acquired Zitara — [Zitara](https://www.zitara.com/resources/zitara-acquired-by-elysia-expanding-battery-intelligence-capabilities)

### Inferences
- **Failure modes to plan against:**
  1. **"Me-too SoH read":** reselling the BMS number has no defensible value once Article 14 access is routine and free apps exist.
  2. **Coverage debt:** a solo founder cannot keep up with protocol changes across dozens of models and software updates.
  3. **Channel lock-out:** pan-European leasers (Arval with Moba) and ACL (Aviloo) may already have group contracts.
  4. **Liability:** a wrong SoH changes a car's price, so disputes need PI insurance and clear accuracy statements.
  5. **Small-country volume ceiling.**
  6. **Burnout** from combining field testing with software development alone.
- **Defensible positions a solo founder can realistically hold:**
  - (a) Cross-brand normalised SoH and remaining-useful-life estimates, going beyond the BMS number, backed by a published validation. This fits the ACEA point that raw BMS values are not comparable.
  - (b) Fleet and leasing analytics built on telematics and API data, a software model that scales without technician visits.
  - (c) Being the local Luxembourg/Greater Region operator for a larger vendor while building those assets.
  - (d) Niches the big players under-serve: vans and light commercial vehicles, plug-in hybrids, or pre-purchase inspections bundled with used-EV marketplaces.

### Gaps
- No documented shutdowns of small SoH-testing startups were found. Search budget ran out before searching failure cases, so the failure modes above are inferred, not evidenced.
- The Battery passport start date (widely cited as February 2027) and Euro 7 battery-durability monitoring requirements were not verified in this session.
- No sourced legal analysis of liability for SoH certificates in Luxembourg.

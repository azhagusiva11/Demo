## 1. How large is the used-EV market in Europe, how fast is it growing, and how many EVs come off lease 2025–2028?

### Takeaway
Used-BEV sales in Europe's big markets are growing 30–50% a year from a small base (about 3–4% of used-car transactions). In 2025 the UK, Germany, France and the Netherlands together saw roughly 0.75–0.8 million used-BEV transactions, about twice the US total (378k). No credible pan-European count of off-lease EVs for 2025–2028 was found. What we know: the 2022–2023 fleet-driven BEV cohorts are now being remarketed, and 2025 EU new-BEV registrations reached 1.88 million, which sets up a larger return wave from 2028 onward.

### Inferences
- **Big-4 used-BEV volume in 2025 was about 0.75–0.8 million transactions.** UK 274,815 + France 178,879 + Germany about 225,000 (the Jan–Oct 189k annualised) + Netherlands about 90–100k (H1 45,726 doubled). That is roughly 2x US used-EV volume (378k). This counts retail ownership changes only. B2B wholesale trades of the same car (lease return to auction to dealer) mean more "test opportunities" than retail transactions.
- **Growth runs at +30–50% a year** in all four markets, with used BEV at about 3.3–4.3% of used transactions. If growth continues, used BEV reaches 6–10% of used transactions in these markets by 2028.
- **Off-lease wave (estimate, needs verification).** Roughly 60% of EU new BEVs go to corporate or fleet buyers. With 3–4-year terms, the 2022–2023 EU BEV cohorts (from my prior knowledge of ACEA data, about 1.1m and 1.5m; not re-verified this session) imply roughly 0.6–0.9m BEVs a year returning from leases and fleets in 2025–2027. The 1.88m 2025 cohort points to more than 1m a year from about 2028–2029. The report should state this as an estimate.
- Market-research "USD 63bn" market-size figures describe used-EV transaction value, not SoH-service revenue, and come from a vendor with an evident data error. Do not use them as SoH-market sizing.

## 2. Who actually pays for SoH reports, and what do they pay?

### Takeaway
The paying demand is mainly **B2B, on the seller side**. Leasing companies (Ayvens, Arval) and remarketing/auction houses (BCA, Copart, SVA, Manheim) put SoH on ex-fleet EVs to cut the "uncertainty discount" at wholesale. Dealers and dealer associations are the next layer (ZDK, BOVAG, mobile.de dealers). Private buyers and sellers pay about €79–150 per consumer test, but volumes are small. OEM certified-pre-owned programmes and marketplaces are more likely to **bundle or give reports away** than to pay third parties at retail prices. No evidence was found of banks, lenders or insurers paying for SoH at scale.

### Cited Findings
**Leasing companies (paying to certify their own remarketed EVs)**

Live in CZ, FI, FR, DE, PT and NL, with rollout to "most remaining European countries" and the UK to follow. Diagnostics come from CARA-accredited providers and **read SoH from the BMS**. Certificates show capacity as % of original plus real driving range — [Ayvens press release](https://www.ayvens.com/en-cp/news/newsroom/press-releases-2025/ayvens-carmarket-launches-battery-soh-certification-for-used-evs/); [PR PDF dated 25-09-25](https://www.ayvens.com/-/media/ayvens/public/cp/files/news-press-release-pdfs/25-09-25_pr-ayvens-carmarket-launches-battery-state-of-health-certification-for-used-evs.pdf?rev=d185924385ba4c81a8703f4828edca21); follow-up coverage April 2026: [newmobility.news](https://newmobility.news/en/2026/04/08/ayvens-aims-to-unlock-trust-in-used-evs-with-battery-health-certificates/)

### Inferences
- **The strongest willingness to pay sits with sellers holding many EVs:** leasing and rental defleeters and auctions. For them a 1–2% price gain or 2–3 days faster sale on a €15–25k asset is worth far more than a €30–100 test. Their procurement is concentrated: a few leasing majors plus a few auction groups, and many already hold contracts (Ayvens and Arval with Aviloo and Moba, BCA and Copart with Aviloo, SVA with Generational).
- The UK coalition's push to make SoH mandatory for dealer sales and Belgium's Car-Pass move (Q5) point to demand shifting from voluntary to compliance-driven. That would enlarge volume but put pressure on price.
- Most European B2B tenders are already won by Aviloo (the clear incumbent), Moba, Generational and DEKRA. A newcomer in Luxembourg or Benelux would compete against incumbents already under contract with Ayvens, Arval and BCA.

### Gaps
- No public price paid per vehicle by Ayvens, Arval, BCA or Copart. Whether the seller or the buyer pays at BCA was not captured.
- AutoScout24, AutoUncle, Autorola, LeasePlan/Leasys and Alphabet: no SoH programme details found. Search budget ran out.
- Lenders, banks and insurers: no evidence found either way.
- European rental companies (Sixt, Europcar) buying SoH: not found.
- OEM CPO programmes (VW Das WeltAuto, BMW, Renault, Stellantis Spoticar) and whether they use third-party SoH or in-house BMS readouts: not established.

## 3. Price points per test, B2B/fleet pricing, and evidence of willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Consumer-facing tests sell for about **€79–150**: Moba kit €79, Aviloo Premium €99, DEKRA about €100–150. B2B quick tests have dropped to about **US$35 per test** (Aviloo Flash on a monthly B2B plan). Vendors now **bundle a free €3,000 purchase warranty** with a qualifying test to defend that price. Hard evidence of a price premium exists but is modest. BCA's 22,000-car dataset shows +1.4% (£258) and 2.7 days faster sale. Larger claims (€550–1,100 or ~20% premiums, +$1,400) come from vendors or are anecdotal.

### Inferences
- **Unit economics are compressing.** B2B test prices at about $35 (roughly €30–35) and a free €3,000 warranty bundle suggest price competition among incumbents. A new entrant should not plan on €99 consumer pricing as its main B2B price.
- **Rough market sizing (inference, not sourced as a figure):** about 0.8m retail used-BEV transactions in UK/DE/FR/NL in 2025, plus perhaps 0.3–0.6m wholesale ex-fleet tests. At €30–100 per test, that is about €30–140m a year of test revenue potential in these four markets **if every car were tested**. Current penetration is 2% to 20%+ on mobile.de, so the actual 2025 spend is probably a small fraction of that. It could grow 30–50% a year alongside volumes.
- The robust, non-vendor-promoted price effect is about **1–2% of vehicle value** (BCA). On a €20k used EV that is €200–400, which comfortably covers a €30–100 test for high-volume sellers but is thin for private sellers.

## 4. How does battery uncertainty affect used-EV residual values and leasing-company losses?

### Takeaway
EV residual values fell much harder than ICE values in 2023–2025. The main drivers were new-EV price cuts and discounting. Battery uncertainty added an "uncertainty discount" on top. The quantified losses fall mainly on US rental (Hertz about $440m) and UK leasing (BVRLA "heavy losses"). Continental leasing majors (Ayvens, Arval) reported recovering used-car results in 2025, calling EV weakness a contained drag because BEVs were still a small share of disposals. That exposure grows as the 2022–2025 BEV cohorts return.

### Inferences
- Most of the EV residual-value collapse came from **new-car pricing** (Tesla cuts, OEM discounting, incentive changes), not battery degradation, since measured SoH is typically 93–95%. The business case should frame SoH as **risk reduction and liquidity** (faster sale, fewer disputes, lender and warranty enablement), not as recovering the 30–50% value gap.
- Leasing companies have a strong incentive to certify: a 1.4% uplift on tens of thousands of disposals a year per major is worth several million euros. They are also pushed to secure the **lowest per-test price** and to use BMS readouts where possible (as at Ayvens Carmarket).
- As BEV disposals rise from about 5% (Arval) toward 15–25% of leasing remarketing volumes as the 2023–2025 cohorts return, RV risk and SoH demand at leasing companies should grow sharply in 2026–2029.

## 5. Is demand growing, or being eroded (OEM in-car SoH, free marketplace reports, regulation)?

### Takeaway
**Volume demand is clearly growing.** Certificate penetration on mobile.de went from under 5% to over 20% in about 18 months. Leasing majors and auctions adopted SoH during 2024–2026, Belgium is putting SoH on the Car-Pass, and the UK has a push to mandate it. **Standalone paid reports face structural erosion:** OEM on-board SoH monitors (Euro 7 / UN GTR 22), the EU battery passport from Feb 2027, free platform-integrated scores (Cox/Manheim/Autotrader US, EV TrustSeal, Recurrent owner tier) and BMS-readout certificates (Ayvens). Value is shifting from "a number" to **independent verification, standardisation (CARA) and warranties**.

### Cited Findings
**Growth signals**
- mobile.de: certificate share went from under 5% (mid-2024) to more than 20% (about Nov 2025), and from 1 Nov 2025 an upload is required when the certificate box is ticked — [heise](https://www.heise.de/news/Batteriezertifikate-fuer-Elektroautos-Manchmal-nun-Pflicht-bei-mobile-de-11072672.html)

- **Free or in-house scores:** Cox builds its own VIN-specific battery health for Manheim (41,000+ EVs in 2025) and Autotrader US (19,000+ listings, Jan 2025) — [Cox](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights-hub/cox-automotive-deploying-industry-first-vin-specific-ev-battery-health-solution-across-manheim-locations/); [Autotrader US](https://press.autotrader.com/2025-01-20-EV-Battery-Health-Scores-Now-Available-on-Autotrader-Listings). EV TrustSeal is free to consumers — [EV TrustSeal](https://evtrustseal.com/). Recurrent has a free owner tier — [Recurrent](https://www.recurrentauto.com/for-owners). mobile.de displays SoH % in the listing — [mobile.de](https://www.mobile.de/magazin/artikel/elektromobilitaet/batteriezertifikat)
- **BMS readout as certificate:** Ayvens Carmarket's CARA-accredited certificates read SoH from the BMS — [Ayvens](https://www.ayvens.com/en-cp/news/newsroom/press-releases-2025/ayvens-carmarket-launches-battery-soh-certification-for-used-evs/). By contrast, Aviloo markets Flash as "not a BMS readout" but "a genuine diagnostic" and positions it "independently of the manufacturer's status display" — [Aviloo Certified](https://aviloo.com/en-us/aviloo-certified); [Renewable Energy Magazine](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/electric_hybrid_vehicles/aviloo-extends-battery-warranty-to-14-more-20260907)

- **US policy:** an InsideEVs feature is titled "Vetting a used EV battery was about to get easier. Then Trump happened", which suggests US federal battery-durability or SoH rules were rolled back or stalled. Only the title was captured — [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/features/789263/battery-used-ev-trials-hardship/)

### Inferences
- **Near term (2026–2028):** demand grows. Most used EVs on the road were built before Euro 7 / GTR 22 monitors and before the 2027 passport, OEM in-car SoH displays vary, and legal or market standards (Car-Pass, mobile.de upload rule, CARA approval, a possible UK mandate) are pulling certificates into the transaction.
- **Medium term (2028+):** the bare SoH percentage becomes a free commodity (on-board monitor, passport, marketplace display). Defensible value moves to (a) **independent verification** that OEM BMS numbers are accurate, (b) **standardised, cross-brand certificates** accepted by auctions, marketplaces and Car-Pass, (c) **risk products** (warranties, residual-value guarantees, lender inputs), and (d) data and analytics for RV forecasting (McKinsey's €12bn accuracy prize).
- The US pattern of a dominant platform (Cox) building SoH in-house and giving it away is a warning. In Europe, the equivalents (BCA, mobile.de/Adevinta, AutoScout24, Ayvens Carmarket) could integrate or white-label instead of paying per report.

## 6. Luxembourg specifics: EV share, company cars and leasing, used-EV market, cross-border trade

### Takeaway
Luxembourg has high BEV penetration (**26.9% of new registrations in 2025**; 12,664 new BEVs) and a **company-car-dominated market** (54% of new registrations). Fiscal rules strongly favour BEVs (BIK 0.5–0.6% vs 2% for ICE/hybrid). It is one of few countries subsidising **used** BEVs (€1,500). ### Inferences
- **Domestic volume is tiny.** About 12.7k new BEVs a year, with more than half company or leased cars on about 3–4-year terms, implies roughly 3–7k BEVs a year leaving Luxembourg fleets in 2026–2029 (estimate). Even at 100% test penetration and €50–100 per test, domestic Luxembourg SoH revenue is at most a few hundred thousand euros a year. A viable Luxembourg-based business must sell across Benelux, the Greater Region (Lorraine, Saarland, Rhineland-Palatinate, Wallonia) or pan-EU B2B (leasing HQs, remarketing platforms).
- Luxembourg's leasing-heavy market and cross-border export of ex-lease cars fit **seller-side certification at defleet**. Certificates matter most when a car crosses borders to a foreign trade buyer who cannot inspect it. The customers are the Luxembourg leasing subsidiaries (Ayvens, Leasys, Arval/BGL, Alphabet, Athlon), but group-level contracts may already cover them.
- The €1,500 used-BEV subsidy could provide a policy hook: a proposal to make it conditional on an independent SoH certificate. This is speculative and has no evidence of government interest.
